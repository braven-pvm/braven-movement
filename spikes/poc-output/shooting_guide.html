Field Capture Card

What to film so the footage can actually be measured. Every number here was measured by running the recovery on a known pose, not estimated. Get the separation wrong and the trip produces footage we can only look at.

The one thing that matters most

Two phones, 60° to 90° apart, about 3 m from the athlete, at chest height. Both recording at the same time, both seeing the whole body including the feet.

Two phones side by side are no better than one phone. The whole benefit comes from the angle between them.

Two things that turned out not to matter

  • Camera height. Anything from knee height to overhead gave the same result, within a quarter of a degree. Chest height on a tripod is fine. Do not spend time levelling.
  • A third camera. Three phones improved the mean from 2.2° to 1.8°. Not worth the extra setup. Two, placed well, beats three placed badly.

On the day

  1. Set both phones on tripods
    60° to 90° apart, roughly 3 m out, chest height, landscape or portrait, but both the same. Lock the exposure and focus if the phone allows it.
  2. Do not zoom, and do not switch lenses
    Use the plain 1× main camera on both. Zooming changes the lens maths and I cannot recover it afterwards. Note the phone model for each.
  3. Fill the frame with the athlete
    Head and feet in shot, with a little room to spare. The bigger she is in frame, the smaller the error. This is the cheapest accuracy you will get all day.
  4. Film the calibration sheet first
    Hold a sheet of A4 paper flat, facing each camera in turn, for five seconds, standing where the athlete will stand. A printed chessboard is better if you have one. This gives me the real lens instead of a guess.
  5. Start both recordings, then clap once
    One sharp clap in view of both cameras. That is how the two clips get lined up. Repeat the clap if you restart either phone.
  6. Measure the athlete
    Standing height and arm span, fingertip to fingertip, in centimetres. Without height there is no scale, and every movement in the engine is expressed in arm lengths.
  7. Film the drill three times
    Three clean repetitions, with a pause between them. A second athlete of a different height is a bonus, because it tests whether a movement retargets.

What to film

In manual order. The first is the one already built, so it is the direct comparison. The rest extend the library.

  • 2 hand snatches and pull in — the built one. Film this first.
  • 2 hand catch at the chest — arms almost straight, the contrast case.
  • 2 hand snatch straight back — catch and release, no pull in.
  • 1 hand snatch pull in — tests the left and right handling properly.
  • Any one power position hold, still, for five seconds. A static reference is worth a lot for checking the stance.

Mistakes that cost the trip

Any one of these and the footage cannot be measured

  • Phones less than 45° apart. This is the big one.
  • Feet out of frame, or cut off at the knee.
  • Only one phone recording during a take.
  • Zooming, or the phone switching to the ultra-wide lens by itself.
  • No clap, so the clips cannot be synchronised.
  • Height not written down.

Before you leave the field

Figures from spike I, run on this codebase: a known pose is projected to the cameras under test, 2 px of detector noise is added, the pose is recovered from the pixels alone, and the recovered joint angles are compared against the truth. The clinical threshold for a meaningful difference is 5 degrees.
